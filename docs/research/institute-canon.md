# The Institute — Canon Research Notes (Red Rising, Book 1)

Source: *Red Rising*, Pierce Brown (2014, Hodder & Stoughton paperback). Page numbers below are
**PDF page numbers** of the reference scan, not printed page numbers. The scan itself is copyrighted
and is **never committed to this repo**; these notes paraphrase and cite.

Confidence legend: **[C]** canon (stated in text or on the book's printed map) ·
**[I]** inferred from textual clues · **[A]** our artistic invention (must not contradict C).

---

## 1. Where it is

- **[C]** A remote terraformed valley in the **southernmost part of the Valles Marineris** on Mars (p.164).
  Not to be confused with the white-stone, Roman-style Institute complex on the canyon wall beyond
  Agea's night districts, where students arrive and undergo the Passage (p.134). The book's own map
  inset labels the valley region "The Institute" east of Agea.
- **[C]** Canyon walls stand **~50 km west** and **~40 km east** of House Mars's valley, **six km high**
  (p.166). The arrival chapter calls the Marineris walls "eight-kilometer-high" (p.134) — use 6 km for the
  battlefield walls. → playable valley is **~90 km wide**; north–south extent unstated **[A: ~120 km]**.
- **[C]** Gravity .37g (affects nothing visual except the sense of scale/leaping).
- **[C]** Fitchner says "twelve Houses in twelve castles" (p.164) and ~1,200 students arrive. **But the text names
  thirteen Houses with students** (sigil rings of dead students, slaves, banners): Mars, Minerva, Diana, Apollo,
  Ceres, Jupiter, Juno, Pluto, Vulcan, Venus, Mercury, Neptune, Bacchus. The printed map shows exactly **12
  castles** (7 named + 5 unknown triangles); **Pluto's subterranean castle is the 13th, the one not on the map**.
  We render all 13 and turn the discrepancy into a lore note ("the House that isn't on the map").

## 2. The book's printed map (front matter, PDF pp.10–11)

North is up. Two-page spread: left page = west half, right page = east half.

| Region | Position | Contents |
|---|---|---|
| **Mountains** | entire west edge, N→S | Snowy range. **House Jupiter** castle in the NW peaks. One unknown-House triangle mid-west. |
| **North Woods** | top centre | Conifer forest straddling the Argos's headwaters. |
| **Argos River** | centre-west, flows N→S | Two headwater branches join near the top; flows south and empties into the South Sea. |
| **House Juno** | west of the Argos, plains, mid-north | On a tributary of the Argos, framed by mountains (p.320). |
| **House Ceres** | **on** the Argos, north-centre | "River fortress" spanning the river; crops & orchards; at a confluence. |
| **Plains / lowlands** | both banks of the Argos | Two unknown-House triangles, one each side of the river. |
| **Highlands** | north-centre-east | **House Mars** castle + two towers (**Phobos**, **Deimos**) and the Metas/Furor rivers. |
| **Postern Hills** | north-east | **House Minerva** castle. |
| **Greatwoods** | centre-east → south-east | **House Diana** castle (north edge of forest), **House Apollo** castle (far SE). |

Legend shows sigils for Apollo (lyre/trident-like), Ceres (sickle & wheat), Diana (stag), Juno (peacock),
Jupiter (crossed lightning), Mars (wolf), Minerva (owl), and "Unknown House" (empty triangle).
Castles are drawn as a black battlement silhouette over a downward-pointing triangle.
**We design our own sigils** inspired by these — we do not trace the book's artwork.

## 3. House Mars territory (best-documented area)

- **[C] Mars Castle** (p.166): on a hill **just north of the Metas river**, in the middle of a **bowl-like glen**
  (half grass, half woods); larger hills cup the glen in a semicircle N and S.
  - Three walls stand atop **80 m stone cliffs**; walls rise **30 m**; gatehouse swells out as a turreted fortress.
  - **Square keep** built into the **north-west wall**, **50 m** tall; **western gate** opposite the keep,
    reached by a gentle slope and a lonely dirt road up from the glen floor.
  - "Four spires" (p.~190); long dormitories with bunks; a square; a kitchen; a stone map room.
- **[C] Highland character**: mossy hills, craggy peaks, U-shaped grassy glens, heavy mist, fir/oak woods
  "like homespun quilts" on foothills, lochs, gulches, goats, sheep, deerling, wolves, ravens, vultures.
- **[C] Rivers**: **Furor** (east) and **Metas** (west) form a **V pointing south-west**, then join and flow
  to the lowlands as one tributary of the Argos. **A single bridge** spans the Metas — the "open front door"
  (flat, wooded approach from the lowlands to Mars's gate) (p.168–169).
- **[C] Phobos Tower**: stone tower on a rocky summit **~5 km SW** of the castle, in the far SW of the
  territory; view south over the Argos plain, west to the snowy range, SE to the Greatwoods (p.168).
- **[C] Deimos Tower**: massive tower on a dwarf-mountain range **NW**, just beyond the river junction.
- **[C]** A weathered **stone fort on a low hill guarding a pass**, ~10 km from the castle, holding a supply cache (p.183).
- **[C]** Ceres's river fortress is visible to the south from Phobos; the plain between is ~8 km of open grass.

## 5. Olympus

- **[C]** An **artificial floating mountain** hovering **~2 km** up ("nearly a mile above the Argos") where the
  Proctors watch the game (p.168, p.370).
- **[C]** No doors, no dock. **Snow-covered slopes**; peak hidden by cloud; a **bone-pale marble citadel**
  "like a marble sword" at the top; high balconies and stone terraces; dining hall, golden suites, baths,
  holoImmersion rooms; a "fairy-tale castle" glimmer seen from below (p.168, p.370).
- **[C]** From its terrace you can see: the misty lands of Mars, the rocky hills & fields of Minerva, the Greatwoods
  of Diana, the mountains of Jupiter (p.370).

## 6. Time & seasons (useful for a timeline layer)

- **[C]** Summer at the start (mist, showers, bugs) → **winter**: deep snow, frozen Argos crossable on foot,
  "wintry" canyon walls → **thaw**: the Argos thaws unexpectedly fast, stranding armies.
- **[C]** Key beats: Mars–Ceres summer siege · Mars tribal collapse · Darrow takes Minerva & traps Diana ·
  winter march south · fall of Ceres, Apollo, Jupiter · storming of Olympus.

## 7. Additions from T09 research (verified by the lead where marked ✓)

- **Proctors:** only Fitchner (Mars) is named; the rest go by their House god (p.140, 168). Jupiter: a lion-haired
  giant with a lightning-bolt collar. Apollo: bow collar, coiled hair. Mercury: winged feet, baby face (p.139).
  Neptune: trident headdress (p.257).
- **Apollo Castle** ✓: tall, white, ornate. A round plaza with six doorways leading to six spiralling towers, and a
  hundred-metre parapet (p.340). Ramparts look over the Greatwoods to Mars's highlands in the north (p.345). Primus: Novas (p.321).
- **Jupiter Castle** ✓: high rocky ground between two huge rock faces; three-tiered walls bow outward; postern gate (p.353).
- **Minerva Castle:** sandstone, in olive country; 30 m walls; a 100 m mud killing field; a city inside; a war room on
  the third level with an atlas (p.217–218, 243, 254, 259–261). It falls in autumn and is burned (p.259, 261).
- **Ceres Castle:** a courtyard leading to several buildings and a huge keep where the bread bakes (p.309).
- **Deimos** ✓: from afar a dwarf-mountain range (p.168); up close, low hills surrounded by thick woods (p.207).
  The Metas bridge is "near Phobos" (p.301). The Furor is north of the castle (p.207).
- **Winter at Mars** ✓: while Darrow lies wounded for four weeks in a cave in the North Woods, "Cassius is Primus.
  Winter has come." (p.280). Darrow returns only at the thaw (p.383–384).
- **Thaw at Mars:** catapults reduce Phobos and Deimos to rubble; Jupiter, Apollo and a quarter of Pluto besiege the
  castle (p.383–384).
- **Pluto:** subterranean in the low mountains; blitzed by Vulcan on day 3; the Jackal collapsed the tunnels (p.335).
  Scouts ride black horses, with black-dyed hair and bone in their braids (p.264, 320–321, 325). The Jackal is Adrius
  au Augustus, Mustang's (Virginia's) brother (p.382, 389).
- **Names:** "Postern Hills" appears only on the printed map; the text says "Minerva's country of high grass and olive
  trees" (p.254). The text says "southern sea" (p.321) where the map says "South Sea". Bacchus's barrels hold grape
  juice, not wine (p.352, 360).
